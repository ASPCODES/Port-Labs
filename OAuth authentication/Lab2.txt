// Lab: Forced OAuth profile linking


This lab gives you the option to attach a social media profile to your account so that you can log in via OAuth instead of using the normal username and password. Due to the insecure implementation of the OAuth flow by the client application, an attacker can manipulate this functionality to obtain access to other users' accounts.

To solve the lab, use a CSRF attack to attach your own social media profile to the admin user's account on the blog website, then access the admin panel and delete carlos.

The admin user will open anything you send from the exploit server and they always have an active session on the blog website.

You can log in to your own accounts using the following credentials:

Blog website account: wiener:peter
Social media profile: peter.wiener:hotdog





// Solution:


## Step 1

Open the lab.

---

## Step 2

Click **My Account**.

---

## Step 3

Login using the **normal login form** (do NOT use social login).

```
Username: wiener
Password: peter
```

---

## Step 4

After login, click **Attach a social profile**.

---

## Step 5

You will be redirected to the social media website.

Login there with:

```
Username: wiener
Password: peter
```

---

## Step 6

After successful login, you will return to the blog.

Your social account is now linked to your account.

---

## Step 7

Click **Log out**.

---

## Step 8

Click **My Account** again.

Now click **Log in with social media**.

You should be logged in instantly.

(This confirms that your social account is linked.)

---

## Step 9

Go to **Burp → Proxy → HTTP History**.

Find the request:

```
GET /auth?client_id=...
```

Open it and notice:

```
redirect_uri=/oauth-linking
```

There is **no `state` parameter**.

This is the vulnerability.

---

## Step 10

Turn **Intercept ON** in Burp.

---

## Step 11

Go back to the website.

Click **Attach a social profile** again.

---

## Step 12

Burp will intercept several requests.

Keep clicking **Forward** until you see:

```
GET /oauth-linking?code=XXXXXXXX
```

---

## Step 13

Right-click this request.

Select:

```
Copy URL
```

The copied URL will look like:

```
https://YOUR-LAB-ID.web-security-academy.net/oauth-linking?code=XXXXXXXX
```

---

## Step 14

Now click **Drop**.

**Do NOT Forward this request.**

Dropping it keeps the OAuth code valid.

---

## Step 15

Turn **Intercept OFF**.

---

## Step 16

Log out from the blog.

---

## Step 17

Open the **Exploit Server**.

---

## Step 18

In the Body section, write:

```html
<iframe src="PASTE_COPIED_URL_HERE"></iframe>
```

Example:

```html
<iframe src="https://YOUR-LAB-ID.web-security-academy.net/oauth-linking?code=abc123xyz"></iframe>
```

---

## Step 19

Click **Store**.

---

## Step 20

Click **Deliver exploit to victim**.

The victim's browser automatically loads the iframe.

The victim unknowingly links **your social media account** to **their account (Admin)**.

---

## Step 21

Go back to the blog.

Click:

```
Log in with social media
```

---

## Step 22

You will now be logged in as **Administrator**.

---

## Step 23

Open the **Admin Panel**.

---

## Step 24

Delete the user:

```
carlos
```

---

## Step 25

The lab is solved. ✅
